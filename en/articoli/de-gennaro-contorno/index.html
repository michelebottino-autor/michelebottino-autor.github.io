Gianni De Gennaro and Salvatore Contorno, from the 1989 parliamentary record to the 2025 telephone call

Michele Bottino · 27 November 2026

Correction and update · Italian original

English translation of the Italian article dated 27 November 2026. Quotations are translated from the Italian text; consult the original for their exact wording.

On 15 May 2025, in Rome, before meeting the Caltanissetta prosecutors, Salvatore Contorno telephoned Francesco Pacetto, a retired police inspector. He indicated that he intended to get the meeting over with quickly. “Make sure you do,” Pacetto replied. While Contorno was being interviewed, the former inspector used his wife’s phone to inform Gianni De Gennaro’s secretary that Contorno was with the prosecutors and would not answer. The woman said that she would pass on the message. The published account of the conversation ends with that reply. [2]

Public prosecutor Pasquale Pacifico wanted to know whether Contorno was prepared to speak about his own arrest in 1989 and about Arnaldo La Barbera, then head of the Palermo Flying Squad. Contorno replied that, for the time being, he did not intend to do so. The question brought the former cooperating witness back to an affair about which Parliament had already heard his testimony. [1] [2]

The 1989 precedent

De Gennaro headed the police’s Central Anti-Crime Unit. Contorno, formerly a member of Cosa Nostra, had cooperated with the judicial authorities. His return to Sicily and subsequent arrest prompted the Parliamentary Anti-Mafia Commission to reconstruct his movements and his relationships with the officials assigned to monitor him. Among the materials obtained were recordings of Contorno’s telephone calls with the Anti-Crime Unit. One of the people on the other end of the line is named in the parliamentary record: Gianni De Gennaro. [1]

On 9 August 1989, Contorno, De Gennaro and La Barbera were heard. At the sitting of 9 November, rapporteur Giuseppe Azzaro considered their accounts substantially consistent but identified shortcomings in the inquiries conducted after the arrest. Senator Franco Corleone called for a full parliamentary inquiry. On 14 November, the Commission approved Azzaro’s document by a majority vote. [1] [8]

The return from the United States and responsibility for protection

Contorno had begun cooperating with the judicial authorities in 1984. After moving to the United States, the loss of American financial support was, according to his account, the reason for his return. He arrived back in Italy on 18 November 1988. Bianca Guidetti Serra's minority document also records the lifting, on 10 October, of the restriction on residing in Sicily. Protecting the cooperating witness and enforcing the judicial restrictions were separate matters, although some of the same offices were involved. [9] [11]

At the hearing of 9 August, De Gennaro said that around the time of the return the central anti-crime unit had informed the Palermo police headquarters by classified communication. He explained the choice of Palermo as the need for a territorial point of reference without disclosing the actual address. The November 1988 document is thus referred to in an institutional hearing; its original has not been obtained for this account. No further instructions can be attributed to it without examining it. [9]

According to Guidetti Serra, the conditions required periodic telephone contact and notification of changes of residence. The papers examined by the Commission recorded the calls but not notifications concerning the moves. Contorno described two stays in Sicily, explaining that he sought financial assistance from his cousin Gaetano Grado. The dates of the first stay were not established with certainty. [11]

Falcone: a forgotten interview

On 9 August Luciano Violante and Franco Corleone asked Contorno whether Giovanni Falcone had questioned him after his return to Italy. Contorno did not remember; Violante then referred to the record of 10 March 1989. Faced with that reference, Contorno did not dispute that the interview had occurred. De Gennaro confirmed that it had taken place in his office, specifying that he had not been present. [9] [11]

The passage distinguishes three facts: the interview cited by the Commission, the location indicated by De Gennaro and Contorno's uncertain recollection. It does not show that Falcone knew of or authorised the subsequent trips to Sicily. The record of 10 March must be sought as a separate document. [9]

The telephone call of 16 May 1989

De Gennaro told the Commission that on 16 May Arnaldo La Barbera informed him of Contorno's presence in Sicily following an intercepted call. Corleone questioned him about his conversation with Contorno, observing that it went beyond a routine report on his circumstances. [9]

In his minority report Corleone reproduced passages in which De Gennaro asked for news and indications and Contorno replied in terms the senator considered unclear. The issue was the nature of the relationship: a routine requirement to remain contactable, or an exchange of intelligence? The text published by Corleone records his question; it does not prove a clandestine assignment. [10]

De Gennaro's account was different: the Palermo Mobile Squad was investigating Grado and his office had not organised Contorno's journey to Sicily. The witness was arrested on 26 May. In the official's view, that arrest was incompatible with an arrangement designed to shield him from judicial action. The parliamentary discussion concerned precisely the scope of that explanation. [9]

The objections raised by the parliamentary minority

On 9 November Corleone recalled that the working group had carried out preliminary inquiries, not an investigation exercising all the Commission's powers. He therefore requested a full parliamentary inquiry. He cited inadequate controls, the circumstances of the return, the telephone calls and aspects of contemporary judicial decisions. On 24 January 1990 he set out his dissent in the minority report. [10]

Guidetti Serra also raised questions about the management of the witness, the relationship between protection and supervision, and his capacity to provide new information. She recalled that on 24 July 1989 the Termini Imerese court acquitted Contorno of the weapons possession charge for insufficient evidence, a matter separate from the other allegations of the time. [11]

Neither report is a judgment. They do show, however, that Parliament was aware of questions about the witness's management and that not all commissioners considered the November 1989 answer sufficient. The majority conclusion and the objections must be read together. [10] [11]

How De Gennaro’s name resurfaced

In September 2023, Caltanissetta prosecutors were trying to establish what had happened to Paolo Borsellino’s red diary. One line of inquiry led them to search the homes of relatives of La Barbera, who had died in 2002. The diary was not found. In intercepted conversations after the searches, a daughter of the former head of the Flying Squad mentioned De Gennaro several times. Investigators examined the relationship between the two officials more closely. [3]

The meeting between De Gennaro and Pacetto

On 8 April 2025, just over a month before the hearing, De Gennaro met Pacetto at the Centro Studi Americani in Rome. According to the transcript published by LiveSicilia, he said he had learned that investigations in Caltanissetta concerned Totuccio, the name he used for Contorno. He advised caution when using the telephone. He added: “It is better to be informed.” [2]

Pacetto also assisted Contorno with personal and financial matters. According to the prosecution, De Gennaro allegedly used the former inspector to monitor Contorno and induce him to remain silent. Contorno’s lawyer offered a different explanation: his client, who felt abandoned by the State, had refused to speak to prosecutors for years. [4] [5]

Preliminary investigations judge Giulia Zappalà acknowledged De Gennaro’s interest in what Contorno might report. She noted, however, that Contorno, interviewed with the safeguards applicable to his legal position, could choose not to answer. She considered the indications of pressure or of a concrete promise insufficient, as well as those intended to establish that Pacetto acted on De Gennaro’s instructions. In November 2026, she rejected the application for house arrest. The prosecution appealed. [4] [6]

The papers kept by De Gennaro

In October 2024, a bag was stolen which, according to investigators, contained material from De Gennaro’s archive. The Carabinieri’s ROS recovered some of the documents in Rome, but not the hard drive. Among approximately 850 pages examined were press cuttings, confidential communications and a 1989 Carabinieri report connected with the Contorno case. [6]

On 26 November 2025, according to the account of the judicial order, De Gennaro and his associate Donatella Peruzzi spoke about documents concerning Contorno, scans and papers that had been burned. [6]

According to the prosecution, this conduct was intended to obstruct the investigation. The judge observed that it was not yet clear which papers had been destroyed, whether digital copies existed or whether they concerned the red diary investigation. [6]

De Gennaro died by suicide on 21 November 2026, before the appeal concerning precautionary measures was heard. In the letter he left before his death, he defended his conduct and explained that he had wanted to control who could examine the material in his custody. As of 27 November, inquiries into the disk recovered from his home were continuing. [7]

The rejection of the applications for precautionary measures was not a final decision on the merits of the allegations. The contents of the archive and their possible relevance to the red diary investigation remained to be established. [6]

Developments on 26 and 27 November

On 26 November, the Caltanissetta review court postponed until 17 December the hearing on the precautionary measures sought for Salvatore Contorno, Francesco Pacetto, Donatella Peruzzi and Massimo Carnicelli. The appeal concerns those four individuals. ANSA, 26.11.2026

On 27 November, the autopsy ordered by the Grosseto prosecutor’s office began at Careggi. Prosecutors are investigating possible incitement to suicide by persons unknown. Toxicology tests were also ordered. Il Tirreno, 27.11.2026

The two strands of the current investigation

In the statement of 16 November 2026 reported by Rai, the Caltanissetta prosecution distinguished alleged interference with statements from alleged interference with physical evidence. The first concerned the suspected influence on Contorno; the second the suspected concealment of paper and digital records after the 2023 searches. These are prosecution allegations, not facts established by a judgment. [14]

The distinction matters: proving that people close to De Gennaro communicated does not establish unlawful pressure on Contorno. Similarly, tracing the movement of an archive does not establish that its contents were relevant to the investigation or that someone intended to distort the reconstruction of events. These are evidentiary steps that must be established.

The 527-page report and the letter

On 27 November la Repubblica reported that a 527-page investigative report had been submitted on 29 July and filed with the review court. According to the newspaper, the proposed inquiries include further interviews, a review of intercepted conversations and examination of evidence and digital devices. The complete report is not among the sources examined here, and no definitive conclusions can be attributed to it. [12]

The same newspaper reported that the prosecutors were still awaiting official transmission of De Gennaro's final letter, in which the former police chief rejected allegations of disloyalty and referred to errors and orders received. The incidents or people to which those words refer have not been identified. The letter alone does not establish an admission of a criminal offence. [12]

Open also reported further inquiries into confidential messages, digital devices and relations between De Gennaro and Falcone. These too are developments reported by the press, not established findings. Verification requires the records and, where possible, the full statements cited. [13]

What connects 1989 and 2026

The documented connection runs through Contorno, the 1989 hearings and the question put to him by prosecutors in May 2025 about his arrest and La Barbera. The records examined here do not establish criminal continuity between those events and the alleged obstruction now under investigation. Bruno Contrada belongs to a separate investigative history: historical and institutional proximity is not evidence of involvement in this case. [1] [2] [9]

What remains to be established is what Contorno knew about the return to Sicily, what information circulated between offices in 1989, which documents actually survived and why their contents were considered relevant to the new investigation. Answers require the classified communication of 1988, the record of the 10 March interview, complete telephone recordings and contemporary records not yet public. A sequence of events does not establish a connection; documents must prove it.
