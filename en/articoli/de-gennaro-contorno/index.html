Gianni De Gennaro and Salvatore Contorno, from the 1989 parliamentary record to the 2025 telephone call

Michele Bottino · 27 November 2026

Correction and update · Italian original

English translation of the Italian article dated 27 November 2026. Quotations are translated from the Italian text; consult the original for their exact wording.

On 15 May 2025, in Rome, before meeting the Caltanissetta prosecutors, Salvatore Contorno telephoned Francesco Pacetto, a retired police inspector. He indicated that he intended to get the meeting over with quickly. “Make sure you do,” Pacetto replied. While Contorno was being interviewed, the former inspector used his wife’s phone to inform Gianni De Gennaro’s secretary that Contorno was with the prosecutors and would not answer. The woman said that she would pass on the message. The published account of the conversation ends with that reply. [2]

Public prosecutor Pasquale Pacifico wanted to know whether Contorno was prepared to speak about his own arrest in 1989 and about Arnaldo La Barbera, then head of the Palermo Flying Squad. Contorno replied that, for the time being, he did not intend to do so. The question brought the former cooperating witness back to an affair about which Parliament had already heard his testimony. [1] [2]

The 1989 precedent

De Gennaro headed the police’s Central Anti-Crime Unit. Contorno, formerly a member of Cosa Nostra, had cooperated with the judicial authorities. His return to Sicily and subsequent arrest prompted the Parliamentary Anti-Mafia Commission to reconstruct his movements and his relationships with the officials assigned to monitor him. Among the materials obtained were recordings of Contorno’s telephone calls with the Anti-Crime Unit. One of the people on the other end of the line is named in the parliamentary record: Gianni De Gennaro. [1]

On 9 August 1989, Contorno, De Gennaro and La Barbera were heard. At the sitting of 9 November, rapporteur Giuseppe Azzaro considered their accounts substantially consistent but identified shortcomings in the inquiries conducted after the arrest. Senator Franco Corleone called for a full parliamentary inquiry. On 14 November, the Commission approved Azzaro’s document by a majority vote. [1] [8]

How De Gennaro’s name resurfaced

In September 2023, Caltanissetta prosecutors were trying to establish what had happened to Paolo Borsellino’s red diary. One line of inquiry led them to search the homes of relatives of La Barbera, who had died in 2002. The diary was not found. In intercepted conversations after the searches, a daughter of the former head of the Flying Squad mentioned De Gennaro several times. Investigators examined the relationship between the two officials more closely. [3]

The meeting between De Gennaro and Pacetto

On 8 April 2025, just over a month before the hearing, De Gennaro met Pacetto at the Centro Studi Americani in Rome. According to the transcript published by LiveSicilia, he said he had learned that investigations in Caltanissetta concerned Totuccio, the name he used for Contorno. He advised caution when using the telephone. He added: “It is better to be informed.” [2]

Pacetto also assisted Contorno with personal and financial matters. According to the prosecution, De Gennaro allegedly used the former inspector to monitor Contorno and induce him to remain silent. Contorno’s lawyer offered a different explanation: his client, who felt abandoned by the State, had refused to speak to prosecutors for years. [4] [5]

Preliminary investigations judge Giulia Zappalà acknowledged De Gennaro’s interest in what Contorno might report. She noted, however, that Contorno, interviewed with the safeguards applicable to his legal position, could choose not to answer. She considered the indications of pressure or of a concrete promise insufficient, as well as those intended to establish that Pacetto acted on De Gennaro’s instructions. In November 2026, she rejected the application for house arrest. The prosecution appealed. [4] [6]

The papers kept by De Gennaro

In October 2024, a bag was stolen which, according to investigators, contained material from De Gennaro’s archive. The Carabinieri’s ROS recovered some of the documents in Rome, but not the hard drive. Among approximately 850 pages examined were press cuttings, confidential communications and a 1989 Carabinieri report connected with the Contorno case. [6]

On 26 November 2025, according to the account of the judicial order, De Gennaro and his associate Donatella Peruzzi spoke about documents concerning Contorno, scans and papers that had been burned. [6]

According to the prosecution, this conduct was intended to obstruct the investigation. The judge observed that it was not yet clear which papers had been destroyed, whether digital copies existed or whether they concerned the red diary investigation. [6]

De Gennaro died by suicide on 21 November 2026, before the appeal concerning precautionary measures was heard. In the letter he left before his death, he defended his conduct and explained that he had wanted to control who could examine the material in his custody. As of 27 November, inquiries into the disk recovered from his home were continuing. [7]

The rejection of the applications for precautionary measures was not a final decision on the merits of the allegations. The contents of the archive and their possible relevance to the red diary investigation remained to be established. [6]

Developments on 26 and 27 November

On 26 November, the Caltanissetta review court postponed until 17 December the hearing on the precautionary measures sought for Salvatore Contorno, Francesco Pacetto, Donatella Peruzzi and Massimo Carnicelli. The appeal concerns those four individuals. ANSA, 26.11.2026

On 27 November, the autopsy ordered by the Grosseto prosecutor’s office began at Careggi. Prosecutors are investigating possible incitement to suicide by persons unknown. Toxicology tests were also ordered. Il Tirreno, 27.11.2026
