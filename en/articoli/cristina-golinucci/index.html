The Cristina Golinucci case

Documentary reconstruction and structural analysis

Michele Bottino

Italian original

English translation of the Italian original. Time references and assessments reflect the source article, not a new verification of the proceedings.

Classification: Real case / Documentary analysis

Case status: Closed, but capable of being reopened if new evidence emerges

Level of certainty: High for the initial event, low for the chain of evidence

Method applied: Bottino System (MRN)

Tags: Cristina Golinucci, Cesena, Capuchin friary, Ronta, Emanuel Boke, documentary analysis

Update: The proceedings have not produced a definitive resolution. The case remains marked by successive closures, but reopening remains theoretically possible if new, relevant material emerges that can actually be used for investigative or evidentiary purposes.

Cristina Golinucci’s disappearance has left testimony and leads that have not resulted in a conclusive reconstruction. Reports must be assessed according to their actual reliability.

Structural reading of the case

This case is also examined in the series Le strutture dell’irrisolto (The Structures of the Unresolved), devoted to the methodological reading of documentary configurations and forms of non-convergence.

Read the structural analysis of Cristina Golinucci (Italian)

On 1 September 1992, a twenty-one-year-old woman leaves her home in Cesena. She has a specific appointment in a specific place. She is to go to the Capuchin friary in Ronta. Her name is Cristina Golinucci.

She will never arrive.

Her car is found parked in front of the friary. No further trace of her will be found.

Cristina’s destination is known, but it has not been possible to establish with certainty what happened near the friary. It is at this juncture, between the discovery of the car and the young woman’s disappearance, that the difficulties of reconstruction are concentrated.

Cristina had an appointment with a friar, Father Lino. But the friar reports that he did not see her arrive. No one in the original body of testimony confirms with certainty that she entered the friary. There is no direct witness who places her inside.

The presence of the car does not establish that Cristina entered the friary. None of the testimony cited confirms that step.

For a long time, what develops around this gap is not an answer but a narrowing of the problem. In the initial phase, the case is treated in a way that does not fully reflect its unusual nature: voluntary disappearance is discussed, and in some accounts even suicide. This interpretation simplifies the problem rather than broadening its scope.

Only later is the case progressively reconsidered as a possible criminal act.

But this shift does not arise from decisive proof. It comes through reassessment.

Meanwhile, investigations proceed, open, close and reopen. Numerous proceedings follow over the years, culminating in the closure of the tenth investigation in 2024. As of the time of writing, no defendant has been convicted. There is no definitive reconstruction.

The inquiries conducted have not led to a judicial conclusion about Cristina’s fate.

For a long time, the case appears to be a system almost devoid of useful testimony. Over time, however, this reading proves incomplete.

Some testimony is reported only many years after the events, making it particularly important to verify its accuracy and corroboration.

In 2024, for instance, testimony resurfaces that alters the perception of the location. A woman says she saw Cristina in the friary car park arguing with a man. The account introduces a new element: a possible contact, a possible conflict, an interaction at the very point where previously there was only a gap.

During the same period, another, still more complex body of testimony emerges. The daughter of a now elderly man relates what her father is said to have seen in 1992: a friar and a young woman entering a wooded area near Montepetra. The account also refers to foul-smelling black bags found among brambles.

These elements have a powerful impact.

But they have a decisive characteristic:

they emerge late and do not become established.

They do not form a continuous sequence of reconstruction. They remain fragments.

The case, therefore, is not devoid of observations. It is a case in which observations emerge belatedly.

Within this system, over time, one name gains greater prominence than the others: Emanuel Boke.

He represents the greatest concentration of investigative attention in the case. He is linked to the setting of the friary. In subsequent years, he is convicted of sexual assaults committed in the same geographical area. According to the publicly available material, he allegedly confessed to the murder to Father Lino in 1995, only to retract the confession before the authorities.

Added to this is a physical item: a roll of photographic film found in his pockets and developed many years later, containing images of the setting in which the case is situated.

This cluster of elements carries evident weight.

Yet it does not yield a solution.

Because the decisive step is missing: transforming these elements into a stable chain of evidence.

The case, therefore, is not without direction.

It has a direction.

But it cannot consolidate it.

From a structural perspective, the Cristina Golinucci case can be precisely defined.

It is a case in which:

the initial event is certain, the destination is known, the location is defined, observation at the decisive point is missing, testimony emerges late, investigators identify a possible line of inquiry, the evidence fails to form a chain, the judicial process remains inconclusive, and the narrative grows faster than verification.

It is not a case devoid of elements.

It is a case in which the elements do not connect.

There is only one central point.

Cristina Golinucci disappears at the moment when she should have been seen.

And she is not seen.

Everything else stems from this initial absence: fragmented testimony, dispersed investigative efforts, difficulties of proof, and the absence of a final judgment.

The case does not stall at the end.

It stalls at the beginning.

At the time of writing, more than thirty years later, the case remains formally open or capable of being reopened. The family continues to demand the truth. New elements continue to emerge. But the structure of the case is now defined.

Because what is missing is not just an answer.

It is the step that should have produced it.

Not all unresolved cases are the same.

Some remain open because they have not been investigated sufficiently. Some because evidence is lacking. Some because leads run out.

The Cristina Golinucci case is different.

It is a case in which the system has produced elements, reopened inquiries, verified material and repeatedly attempted to reconstruct events.

But it has never succeeded in establishing the point at which everything broke off.

It is not the mystery of what happened afterwards.

It is the mystery of what was not seen while it was happening.
