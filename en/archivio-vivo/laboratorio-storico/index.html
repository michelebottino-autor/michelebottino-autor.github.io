Three issues, one archival route

How can three issues of a 1918 daily newspaper become an exhibition for visitors while keeping provenance, dates, attributions and unresolved questions visible?

Authentic sources3 newspaper pages4 proposed panelsOne newspaper
Essential limitation: all three issues are from The Morgan City Daily Review. They document what the newspaper published. They do not independently confirm three times that relief was provided. The four panels are a design exercise for a proposed exhibition.

Inventory of the small collection

An editor selected three dates from the same newspaper. The resulting corpus is not an archival collection formed by three different creators. The Library of Congress hosts the reproductions in Chronicling America, with contributions from Louisiana State University.

    On this page: the 10 October page reports local measures and the search for volunteers.

    What the page does not establish: The total number of relief operations actually carried out.

  3. MC-14 — 1918-10-14 · page 4

    More Nurses Needed / Shipyard Workers Organize for Relief Official scan · 1918-10-14 · page 4 (JPEG)

    On this page: the 14 October page carries two reports on relief efforts and a fund promoted by shipyard workers.

    What the page does not establish: Whether the collaboration took place or how many people received aid according to independent records.

Storyboard for four panels

  1. 1. The appeal

    The newspaper reported measures and a search for volunteers

    Item: MC-10 · Limitation: No independent account documents the activities announced.

  2. 2. The committee

    The newspaper describes the formation of a committee and reported offers of help

    Item: MC-11 · Limitation: A news report cannot take the place of the organisation’s internal records.

  3. 3. New needs

    Reports on the appeal for nurses and the shipyard workers’ fund appear on the same page

    Item: MC-14 · Limitation: Cooperation with the Red Cross is anticipated. Officials are the source for the account of aid provided.

  4. 4. What is missing

    Administrative records and evidence of implementation still to be located

    Item: open question · Limitation: Missing records prove neither the failure nor the success of the initiative.

Use the fourth panel to show unresolved questions. A gap in the record cannot settle whether the initiative succeeded or failed.

Design assignment

  1. What role does each issue play in the proposed chronology?
  2. How would you cite the date, page, heading, digitising institution and provenance?
  3. Why do three issues of one newspaper not provide three independent checks on the aid?
  4. Which sentences need to attribute the information to the newspaper rather than present it as an established fact?
  5. Which records from a different creator would help verify the interventions and their outcomes?

Submit: A four-panel storyboard with an inventory of the three newspaper items, seven captions or notes, and a section on evidential limits. You can start from the dossier already checked in the Investigative Writing course (IT). The work to be assessed here is your curatorial design, rather than a retelling of the historical article.

Editorial checks

  • The three issues are explicitly identified as coming from one newspaper.
  • Each panel retains a link to the date, page and original resource.
  • The panels distinguish reporting, intention and verified action.
  • Medical advice from 1918 is not presented as a current recommendation.
  • Images are reproduced only after rights have been checked.
