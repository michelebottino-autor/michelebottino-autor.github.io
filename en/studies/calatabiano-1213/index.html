Calatabiano, March 1213

The royal charter concerning the Church of Catania: what the printed text establishes, and what remains unresolved.

Prepared by Michele Bottino · Archivio Documentale Calatabiano (ADC)

Abstract

The printed text of a charter dated at Messina in March 1213 concerns Constance and her son Henry and a royal grant or confirmation in favour of the Church of Catania involving the castle of Calatabiano. This note identifies attestations that can be checked against nineteenth- and twenty-first-century printed editions, distinguishes the narrated purchase from the royal act recording it, and isolates questions requiring examination of the medieval documentary witness. It is a source-critical reading of published material, not a new diplomatic edition of the original charter.

Evidence status: printed editions examined; medieval original not consulted; formal external scholarly review not claimed.

1. Textual transmission and editorial basis

The charter is printed in J.-L.-A. Huillard-Bréholles, Historia diplomatica Friderici secundi, volume I, part I (1852), pp. 253–255 [1]. The ADC has visually checked these three printed pages. Christian Hillen reproduces the relevant documentary tradition in Die Urkunden des Königs Heinrichs (VII.) (2007), document 4, pp. 47–49 [2]. The text layer of the latter edition has been reviewed, but a page-by-page visual comparison of Hillen's printed pages has not been completed.

Huillard-Bréholles also refers to an earlier edition by Pirri, Sicilia sacra, volume I, pp. 533–534 [3]. These editions are links in an editorial tradition, not three independent medieval witnesses. No claim of palaeographical verification of the underlying original can be based on their agreement alone.

2. Statements supported by the printed source

Issuers, beneficiary and dating

The act identifies Constance, Queen of the Romans and of Sicily, together with her son Henry, King of Sicily, and concerns a grant and confirmation to the Church of Catania. The closing dating clause places it at Messina, March 1213, in the first indiction (Huillard-Bréholles, p. 255). No day of the month is supplied in the text examined.

Object of the grant

On p. 254, the edition prints castrum Calatabianum with casalibus, villanis, terris, tenimentis et pertinentiis suis — the castle together with associated settlements or rural holdings, dependent persons, lands, tenures and appurtenances. These are categories in the document: they do not themselves identify individual places, reconstruct boundaries, or establish population totals.

Reported purchase and amount

The narrative refers to comiti Arnaldo and to a payment of 15,000 tarì to the count or his agent, with a typographical lacuna in the transmitted printed passage (p. 254). The royal act relates and confirms a purchase; it is not the separately executed original sale contract. The date of the confirmation must not automatically be treated as the independent date of that transaction.

Other historical actors

The document refers to Pagano and Gualterio de Parisio in a narrative of alleged injuries to the Church of Catania (pp. 253–254). A statement made in the royal narrative is not, by itself, independent corroboration that every allegation occurred as described.

3. Critical distinctions

Arnaldo / Armaleo

The checked printed edition gives Arnaldo. The identification as Armaleo Monaldeschi appears in later scholarship and should be reported as an interpretation, rather than silently substituted into the Latin document. The original witness and its readings still require investigation.

Royal confirmation / sale

The legal form of the surviving published act must be distinguished from the purchase narrated within it. The latter is not independently documented here through an original contract.

Printed source / parchment

Text checked in a scholarly edition is evidence for the wording of that edition. It is not a direct collation of a medieval parchment or proof of the provenance and integrity of its original witness.

4. Position in previous scholarship

Hans Niese had already discussed the relevant claims, the reported purchase, the sum of 15,000 tarì, an alternative figure of 13,000 appearing in another source, administrative actors and jurisdictional distinctions [4]. Modern treatments by Antonio Mursia [5] and Giovan Giuseppe Mellusi [6] describe the 1213 episode through different emphases — respectively, acquisition and restoration of rights or control to the Church of Catania. These summaries need not be incompatible; an exact comparison of legal language and documentary chronology would be required to decide.

The ADC does not claim that these established aspects constitute an original historical discovery. The note's present contribution is an explicit separation of attested editorial wording, legal interpretation and unresolved evidential questions.

5. Outstanding checks

  1. Locate and identify the medieval original or relevant manuscript witness, recording repository, shelfmark and documentary history.
  2. Compare the witness with the printed readings, particularly Arnaldo, the payment clause and any editorial lacunae.
  3. Investigate the sale instrument separately from the royal confirmation, if an independently transmitted record is available.
  4. Reassess the sequence of claims in 1201, 1213 and 1267 only after comparison of their respective primary documentary evidence.

No manuscript images are reproduced here, and no manuscript reading is attributed to a scholar who has not examined the evidence.
