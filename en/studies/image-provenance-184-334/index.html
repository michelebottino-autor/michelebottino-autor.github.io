When two sets of images are not two distinct sources

A documented comparison of nine photographs labelled “Processo 184” with pages of a 20-page study PDF labelled “Processo 334”.

Prepared by Michele Bottino · Archivio Documentale Calatabiano (ADC)

Abstract

File names used during earlier ADC acquisitions suggested that photographs marked P184 and a PDF labelled P334 might represent separate source sets. A digital-image comparison identified nine photographic matches within the study PDF. This result concerns the correspondence of reproductions; it does not authenticate the original documents, assign shelfmarks or validate transcriptions. The nine match records and their geometric consistency counts are made available as CSV data.

1. Research problem

Digital study copies had been assigned names referring to “Processo 184” and “Processo 334”. Such a file name is a local identifier, not an authenticated archival shelfmark. The question was whether the nine photographs showed different documents, or whether their visual content already occurred within a second digital reproduction set.

The ADC's earlier uncertainty register had identified the attribution problem. The subsequent comparison concerns digital copies alone; the originals were not inspected.

2. Method

  1. Take the nine source photographs and the 20-page study PDF as the comparison corpus.
  2. Compare local visual features using SIFT (Scale-Invariant Feature Transform).
  3. Use RANSAC (Random Sample Consensus) to identify geometrically consistent matches between each photograph and the corresponding PDF page.
  4. Record the matching PDF page and the count of geometrically consistent correspondences.
  5. Check archival descriptions separately. Do not infer manuscript attribution or textual accuracy from the image-matching result.

RANSAC counts provide an indication of the geometric strength of a visual comparison. They are not probabilities of historical accuracy and cannot establish the identity of the archival original.

4. What the archival finding aid establishes

The Archivio di Stato di Palermo, Protonotaro del Regno, finding aid no. 122 distinguishes Processo 184, associated in the register with Barnaba Gaetani and Betta Cruillas (1476–1482), from Processo 334, associated with Tommaso Mirulla (1484–1489).

That distinction is a statement about archival series descriptions. It does not prove which underlying original yielded each page in the study copies. The photographic matches and the archival register answer different questions.

5. Open verification tasks

  1. Establish full shelfmarks and folio references for the original case files.
  2. Compare the study reproductions with copies whose archival provenance has been independently confirmed.
  3. Examine handwriting and, where possible, prepare controlled diplomatic transcriptions of selected passages.
  4. Review historical interpretations that rely on the two case files, without treating digital similarity as proof of the content of either original.

The manuscript photographs are not redistributed on this page. The available CSV supports review of matching pairs and counts but does not by itself contain the source images or a complete independently reproducible software environment.
