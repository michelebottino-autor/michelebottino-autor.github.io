A museum in one room

Six units and eighteen lessons on designing a small exhibition around six objects and documenting their descriptions, origins, rights and limitations.

Teaching preview under review. Case B-01 is wholly fictional. No artefact, archive, person or event is presented as authentic. There is no enrolment, payment, human assessment or certificate. No visitor tests have been carried out.

Visual workshop: six objects, six checks

Please note: All the images below are original diagrams made for fictional case B-01. They do not reproduce historical objects, authentic photographs, receipts or recordings. Open each entry to distinguish what the drawing shows from what it cannot establish.

B01 · Simulated document

Ledger

Examine the entry and its limits

Description: Rows and columns in a fictional ledger.

Limits of the evidence: It establishes no names, transactions or opening of an actual shop.

Research question: Which document would you check before reconstructing the shop’s trading activity?

B02 · Simulated graphic reconstruction

Shop sign

Examine the entry and its limits

Description: An illustrated board bearing the Italian word “BOTTEGA” (shop).

Limits of the evidence: It establishes neither the existence nor the trading name of a historical business.

Research question: How would you distinguish an original photograph of a sign from this reconstruction?

B03 · Simulated photographic composition

Photograph

Examine the entry and its limits

Description: A framed illustration shows shelves and a counter.

Limits of the evidence: It does not establish the location, date of exposure, identities or historical use.

Research question: What information would help identify the location and date?

B04 · Simulated accounting document

Receipt

Examine the entry and its limits

Description: A diagram of a sheet with fields and a placeholder amount.

Limits of the evidence: It documents neither a real payment or sale nor the full set of transactions.

Research question: Would you need an acknowledgement of payment or separate corroboration to establish that payment took place?

B05 · Simulated shop object

Tool

Examine the entry and its limits

Description: Outline of a hand tool for teaching purposes.

Limits of the evidence: Its shape cannot identify an owner, an occupation or a definite period.

Research question: Which physical marks would you measure and photograph on an authentic tool?

Recorded testimony

Examine the entry and its limits

Limits of the evidence: It is neither a recording of a real person nor testimony actually collected.

Research question: What permissions and transcripts would a real interview require?

Unit 1 of 6 · preview

The exhibition question: a story in six objects

Objective: Turn a question for visitors into a route through six distinct objects.

1.1 The guiding question

Before choosing objects, ask how you can tell the story of everyday life in a shop without inventing its customers, owners or history. A focused question keeps the exhibition from becoming a set of unrelated curiosities. Write it in the project record. Keep only objects that help answer it.

1.2 Six objects, six roles

B-01 is a wholly fictional shop with a ledger, sign, photograph, receipt, tool and imaginary recorded testimony. These form no authentic collection and do not establish that the shop existed. Assign codes B01–B06 and explain the purpose of each object. Make clear that the teaching display was constructed.

1.3 Choosing a sequence

Open with the exhibition question. Move from sign to tool, then compare ledger, receipt, photograph and testimony. You may choose another order if you explain it. Distinguish the objects’ chronology from the order of the story. A display arrangement need not reflect the archival order of original documents.

Practical exercise

Imagine a room with six objects from a village shop: ledger, sign, photograph, receipt, tool and recorded testimony. Draw the visitor route. Caption one object in up to 80 words, distinguishing description, provenance and interpretation. Identify permissions needed to display third-party material.

Unit 2 of 6 · preview

Inventory and reproduction rights

Objective: Before making reproductions, inventory the objects, record their provenance and list permissions.

2.1 A basic inventory

For each object, record the code, name, material or format, dimensions if known, description, reported custodian and gaps. No dimensions were invented for B-01: mark them “not measured”. A narrative list cannot replace an inventory. Each reproduction must refer back to the item described.

2.2 Custody and copies

Distinguish the preserved object from its digital photograph, a crop and a graphic reconstruction. Record the creator, acquisition date, changes to copies and location of versions. A cropped photograph may suit a display. Identify it as a detail rather than a complete reproduction.

2.3 Rights, privacy and consent

Before publishing an authentic document, consider ownership of the physical item, copyright, protection of identifiable people and consent for any recordings. Physical possession alone does not grant publication rights. While rights are unverified, use a text description or a clearly labelled simulation.

Practical exercise

Complete six B-01 inventory entries with code, description, fictional provenance, rights to check and reproduction status. Mark at least three fields “unconfirmed”.

Unit 3 of 6 · preview

Captions: what we know and what remains open

Objective: Write captions that distinguish evidence, interpretation and uncertainty.

3.1 A caption in three sentences

Use the first sentence to describe what can be seen, the second for documented provenance and dating, and the third for a possible interpretation and its limits. The tool’s shape does not tell you who owned it. B-01 has no originals to compare: each description is a teaching simulation.

3.2 Attributions and open questions

An undated photograph may show a room without establishing that it was a shop open to the public. A sign can show a trading name without revealing when it was displayed. Write “attribution to be verified” if archival records, an inventory, a receipt or independent testimony are missing.

3.3 Revising an unsupported claim

Unsupported: “This receipt proves that all customers paid in 1962.” A cautious B-01 caption: “In this simulation, the receipt would document a single accounting entry. It does not cover all transactions.” Avoid generalising from one object to an entire community or historical period.

Practical exercise

Write three captions for B04, the simulated receipt: one misleading, one accurate and one accessible. Explain in 100 words why a single receipt cannot support a general conclusion.

Unit 4 of 6 · preview

The visitor route, accessibility and multiple voices

Objective: Plan for physical and digital accessibility and represent more than one point of view.

4.1 A route without barriers

Plan a clear sequence, text readable from different heights, room to move and alternatives for visitors unable to approach the cases. A real plan requires an on-site survey and checks against local rules. A drawing of an imaginary room cannot certify accessibility.

4.2 Sound, text and images

Recorded testimony needs a readable transcript. A photograph needs alternative text describing relevant features without inventing emotions. Review automatic captions before treating them as a transcript. The B-01 voice is fictional. Neither attribute it to a real witness nor imitate an identifiable person.

4.3 Voices and perspectives

Ask whose perspective the ledger conveys and whose is missing. An administrative record may document transactions while saying little about workers’ or customers’ experiences. Make documentary absences visible without inventing interviews and presenting them as genuine.

Practical exercise

Plan six stations with alternative texts, an audio transcript and two design choices that remove barriers. State which conditions need measuring on site.

Unit 5 of 6 · preview

A small physical exhibition and a lightweight digital version

Objective: Prepare a small physical exhibition and a lightweight, accessible HTML version.

5.1 From table to display case

Choose one display case or six simulated stations. For each, prepare a caption, inventory code and source-status label. Identify teaching photographs clearly. Check how lighting affects legibility and preservation. Handle or move authentic documents only with appropriate procedures.

5.2 A simple digital edition

Build a simple page that works on a phone. Include a title, the guiding question, six entries and a way back. Avoid features requiring a permanent connection or a mouse. Text alternatives should convey the important information even if images fail to load.

5.3 Metadata and references

Each panel retains the object code, caption, provenance, rights holder and link to the entry. It should remain intelligible if a link fails. Visitors must be able to distinguish the simulation from an authentic archive.

Practical exercise

Draft six panels on paper or in HTML. Give each a code, title, short text, source and evidential limit. Try reading them without the images and record what remains clear.

Unit 6 of 6 · preview

Final prototype and visitor review

Objective: Produce six prototype panels and a review rubric. Report only tests that have taken place.

6.1 Submission dossier

Bring together the guiding question, inventory B01–B06, six captions, visitor route, rights register and accessible alternatives. Label fictional data in the entries and introduction. Complete documentation prepares the ground for visitor testing. It does not by itself establish effectiveness.

6.2 Review criteria

Draft criteria for five areas: source traceability, accuracy of texts, clarity of the route, accessibility and handling of rights. Describe the criteria without implying that a reviewer has applied them. A self-awarded score is neither a learning certificate nor an independent judgement.

6.3 Planning a visitor test

Frame three questions for observation. Can visitors recognise simulated objects? Can they find the source? Can they understand a caption without an oral explanation? Collect feedback only after providing appropriate information and obtaining consent. No real visitor tests are recorded for this project. The plan supplies no results.

Practical exercise

Submit dossier B-01 with the inventory, six captions, visitor route, rights register and three questions for a future visitor test. State explicitly that you have not conducted that test.
