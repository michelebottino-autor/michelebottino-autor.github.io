Letters beyond the handwriting

Six units and eighteen lessons on making verifiable transcriptions of hard-to-read letters, comparing machine readings and compiling a critical apparatus.

Teaching preview under review. Text L-01 is fictional and belongs to no real person. The exercise uses no private letters or authentic facsimiles. There is no enrolment, payment, human assessment or certificate.

A fragment that invites doubt

Car[a?] Lu... / ci ved[?]amo domani

This text was created for the exercise. Brackets and dots mark uncertainty for teaching purposes. They do not represent a validated diplomatic transcription system. The fragment cannot simply be expanded to “Cara Lucia, ci vediamo domani” (“Dear Lucia, we’ll see each other tomorrow”).

Unit 1 of 6 · preview under review

The letter as object and document

Objective: Record the provenance, custody and physical condition of the letter before interpreting the writing.

1.1 Describe first, then read

Record the material, format, folds, numbering and annotations. A note on the back may be a later addition. Check before attributing it to the letter writer. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

1.2 Tracing provenance

Document who keeps the material, how it was found and which copying steps are known. Record any gaps in that history. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

1.3 Original, copy and photograph

Distinguish the original, a complete reproduction and a cropped image. A partial photograph may omit a date, address or marking on the back. Retain a reference to the complete document. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

Practical exercise

Use an entirely fictional letter for L-01. Make an entry that explicitly identifies the material and provenance as simulated. Distinguish observable features from details you cannot establish. Do not attribute it to real people.

Unit 2 of 6 · preview under review

Transcribing without silent corrections

Objective: Distinguish the diplomatic transcription, the reading version and editorial additions.

2.1 Diplomatic transcription

Reproduce the legible sequence. Preserve spelling, punctuation and unexpanded abbreviations. Use and explain consistent signs for missing passages. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

2.2 Marking gaps and uncertain letters

Use consistent notation for an illegible word, an uncertain character and an editorial addition. Do not substitute your best guess for a letter you cannot read. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

2.3 Reading version and critical apparatus

Prepare a readable version separate from the transcription. Every expansion, normalisation or addition should be traceable to its line and position in the reproduction. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

Practical exercise

For the fictional fragment “Car[a?] Lu... / ci ved[?]amo domani”, produce a diplomatic transcription marking uncertainty and a separate reading version. Justify each addition.

Unit 3 of 6 · preview under review

Difficult handwriting, digital tools and OCR

Objective: Use magnification and assisted transcription while checking AI suggestions against the evidence.

3.1 Examining pen strokes

Compare the same letter in different words. Look at joins and slant. Handwriting alone cannot establish the writer’s identity or personality. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

3.2 OCR and automatic transcription

A machine can confuse marks, numbers and names. Keep its suggestion separate from the human reading and check it line by line against the visible document. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

3.3 Correction log

For each discrepancy, record the machine reading, revised reading, location and reason. Leave unresolved readings open, even if one word would make a better story. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

Practical exercise

Compare the hypothetical AI reading “Cara Lucia, ci vediamo domani” with the incomplete example. Mark the words the AI invented or expanded without confirmation.

Unit 4 of 6 · preview under review

Dates, names and words in context

Objective: Distinguish what the letter says from identifications and dates obtained elsewhere.

4.1 The date of the letter and the date of the event

A date at the head of the letter dates the document. A promise to meet does not establish that the meeting happened. Keep separate fields for the two dates. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

4.2 Namesakes and attribution

An abbreviated signature cannot identify a real person on its own. If the attribution comes from a family recollection or later catalogue entry, cite it and state how confident you are. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

4.3 Place names and historical vocabulary

Transcribe a place name or obsolete word faithfully. Give possible modern equivalents in a note. Distinguish a linguistic interpretation from a verified location. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

Practical exercise

In L-01 a fictional letter is headed “14 May” and proposes meeting “tomorrow”. Make an entry distinguishing the letter date from the proposed meeting date. Leave open whether the meeting took place.

Unit 5 of 6 · preview under review

Translation, commentary and protection

Objective: Preserve ambiguity in translation while protecting people and their rights.

5.1 Leaving gaps in translation

Mark uncertainty in the translation when a passage is doubtful. Avoid making a pronoun or a proper name more specific than the original permits. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

5.2 Writing notes with references

In each note, identify the line, claim, verified external source if available, and limits. A cultural explanation alone cannot establish the meaning the writer intended. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

5.3 Privacy and publication

Consider living people, addresses, family information, ownership of the physical document and copyright. Before publishing authentic images or transcriptions, verify that reuse is permitted. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

Practical exercise

Write a 120-word commentary and a teaching translation or paraphrase of L-01, retaining the illegible parts. State which permissions you would check before using a real family letter.

Unit 6 of 6 · preview under review

A critical edition of a letter

Objective: Compile an entry that allows another reader to repeat your reading and assess your editorial choices.

6.1 Title page and essential details

Record the code, supplied title, provenance, dating, physical description, responsibility for the edition and rights status. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

6.2 Text, notes and variant readings

Present a diplomatic transcription, a reading version, any translation, a glossary and an apparatus of uncertain readings. Make each editorial layer easy to distinguish. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

6.3 Final checks and submission

Check the text against the facsimile and review the notation, image references and stated limits. Independent review and testing with readers are still needed before the edition can be called validated. Apply this to the fictional example L-01, distinguishing what is legible from what remains hypothetical.

Practical exercise

Compile an L-01 dossier with a provenance record, diplomatic transcription, reading text, register of uncertainties, commentary, and a review of privacy and rights.
